### 1.4 How the Range cost changes over time (hourly geo stores)

The bytes fetched equal the steps with data in the touched 512-step block × about 800 B (f4, per 16×16 tile) × 18 tiles.
Today block 2 holds 272 data steps (26056–26327; the rest is fill, which compresses to almost nothing), so 3.94 MB.
*Estimate, not measured:* as daily forecast updates extend the axis to the end of the block (t 26567, 2026-11-11T23:00), uo or vo grows to about 512 × 820 × 18 ≈ **7.5 MB**.
If the 72-h window crosses a block boundary (when `(t − 3576·T) mod 512 > 440`), the read needs two blocks, up to about 10 MB per variable for a few days. Then the cost drops back when the next block starts.
The same logic applies to thetao (≈ 580 B/step/tile today) and waves (§3).

## 5. Range read recipe (for the in-page decoder)

For one chunk URL and a wanted raw byte span `[from, to)` (element offset × 4 for f4, × 2 for i2):
1. `GET` with `Range: bytes=0-1039`. That gives the 16-byte blosc header (`nbytes` u32@4, `bsize` u32@8, `cbytes` u32@12, flags u8@2) and the block-start table, `bstarts[b]` = i32@`16+4b`.
   1040 B covers ≤ 256 blocks; the PHY/WAV chunks have 4–8.
2. Needed blocks are `b0 = floor(from/bsize)` … `b1 = floor((to−1)/bsize)`.
   `GET` with `Range: bytes=min(bstarts[b0..b1]) - (end−1)`, where end is the next larger `bstarts` value after `bstarts[b1]` (or `cbytes`). Do not assume blocks are stored in order.
3. Place both responses at their real offsets in a zeroed `Uint8Array(cbytes)` and run the existing `blosc()` with a block filter.
   This is the only decoder change: `if ((b+1)*bsize <= from || b*bsize >= to) continue;`. Then read elements as before.
If the memcpyed flag (`flags & 2`) is set, fetch `16+from … 16+to−1` directly. In `arco2.mjs` this is `getPartial()` plus the `from/to` arguments of `blosc()`.

## 6. Did not work / caveats

- Gap-free daily KD490/ZSD: not in ARCO until about 2026-11-24 (§2.1). There is no ZSD at all in live MED products.
- `_202611` PHY datasets: STAC entries only, no ARCO assets yet. `downsampled4.zarr` does not exist for the PHY stores (403).
- 72-h hourly currents cannot fit 6 MB for uo+vo with any ARCO layout (best: 7.9 MB today, geo + Range). timeChunked is 7–15× worse for multi-hour windows (60 MB whole, 50 MB with my contiguous-range reads).
- `arco2.mjs` `range` uses one contiguous byte span per chunk. That is optimal for geoChunked and single-step timeChunked reads, but not for multi-step timeChunked reads. The `ponytail:` comment in the code marks where to split per step; the recommended paths do not need it.

### 7.1 Wind and pressure: what was measured

- **Truth**: the three coastal airports, Larnaca LCLK, Paphos LCPH, Akrotiri LCRA. Wind (10-min mean, direction, gust groups) from their METARs,
  [Iowa Environmental Mesonet](https://mesonet.agron.iastate.edu/) ASOS/METAR download (public domain, "attributing the IEM would be appreciated").
  Sea-level pressure to 0.1 hPa from their hourly SYNOPs (17609, 17600, 17601) via [OGIMET](https://www.ogimet.com/) (used for checking only, not shipped).
  The nearest report within 10 min of each hour; direction only when the wind is 5 kn or more.
- **Forecasts**: Open-Meteo's previous-runs API (`previous-runs-api.open-meteo.com`): for each hour, the latest run (lead 0–12 h) and the runs
  1, 2 and 3 days before (leads ~24, ~48, ~72 h). 35 days, 2026-09-29 to 2026-11-02: 2 516 station-hours per lead, the same hours for every model.
- **Cells**: `cell_selection=sea`, as the app's forecast grid uses (its numbers are for the sea). A second run with the nearest land cell is in 7.3.
- Models offered for Cyprus and scored: `best_match` (= `icon_seamless` = `icon_eu`, ICON-EU 7 km here), `ecmwf_ifs` (IFS 9 km), `ecmwf_ifs025`,
  `ecmwf_aifs025_single`, `icon_global`, `gfs_seamless`, `meteofrance_arpege_europe`, `meteofrance_arpege_world`, `ukmo_global_deterministic_10km`,
  `gem_global`. `kma_gdps` and `gfs_graphcast025` have no history there; `dmi_harmonie_arome_europe` returned an error.

### 7.3 Caveats (read before quoting the numbers)

- The airports are on land by the sea; the app's cells are over the sea, where the wind is usually stronger. Both chosen models, and most others, are
  biased low against the stations even at their sea cells.
- With the **nearest land cell** instead, the ranking changes: `best_match` is the best single model (2.25 kn at ~24 h), `best_match` + `gem_global`
  the best pair (2.13), and `best_match` + `ecmwf_ifs` is slightly worse than `best_match` alone (2.32). The app shows sea cells, so the choice follows 7.2.
- **Gusts are not verified**: only 5 METAR gust groups fell within 10 min of an hour in 35 days, and the Cyprus SYNOPs carry no gust group.
  The app's gust is the two models' mean, untested.
- 35 days of autumn at 3 stations: a winter of storms could rank the models differently. Re-run the script each season.

### 7.4 Waves and sea temperature: no open observations near Cyprus

- Copernicus Marine In Situ TAC, Mediterranean (`INSITU_MED_PHYBGCWAV_DISCRETE_MYNRT_013_035`, `index_platform.txt` of 2026-11-03): no moored
  buoy reports waves within 30–37.5 N, 26–37 E; the easternmost active wave buoy is Heraklion, Crete (~700 km away). Near Cyprus there are only
  Argo floats (profiles every ~10 days), one drifter east of the box (35.9 E) and ship temperature reports. EMODnet Physics' ERDDAP has nothing
  more. Satellite altimeter wave heights exist (Copernicus `WAVE_GLO_PHY_SWH_L3_NRT_014_001`, public S3) but only as HDF5 files that need a
  NetCDF library, which this project doesn't use: not done.
- So the wave model is chosen by coverage, not measured: the app keeps Open-Meteo's marine `best_match` (Météo-France MFWAM, ~8 km, assimilates
  altimeter wave heights, runs 8 days, enough for the app's 7). DWD EWAM is finer (~5 km) but ends after ~3.8 days; ECMWF WAM (`ecmwf_wam`)
  also runs 8 days. At one point off Limassol on 2026-10-01 the models disagreed by a factor of 2–3 (0.26–0.70 m), so an observation would matter.
- Sea temperature in the marine API comes from one model only (Météo-France currents); the fish map's temperature is the Copernicus satellite
  analysis anyway (§1, SOURCES.md).

- iNaturalist API v1 ignores `fields`: 50 observations were **4.1 MB**. v2 takes the same filters plus `fields` (≈ 0.66 kB a record).

- iNaturalist place ids, for reference: 10289 Cyprus (1 380 such records), 235723 Cyprus Coastal Waters (1 464), 140296 Cyprus island (1 518);
  the box gets 1 549 (it also takes offshore and boat records).
- Licences: CC BY-NC records (the large majority, from iNaturalist users) are fine only while the app is free; the Feed shows each record's
  licence, and the credits (map attribution) name iNaturalist and GBIF observers.

## R13b. Inshore turbidity from Sentinel-2 (round 13 phase 2: `data/area-clarity.js`, built by `scripts/areas/s2-clarity.mjs`)
- **Data**: Copernicus Sentinel-2 L2A (free and open Copernicus licence; "contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2023-2026") from the
  Microsoft Planetary Computer STAC with its anonymous read token (no account; the COG reader of scripts/seabed-s2/lib.mjs, own cache
  `scripts/areas/cache/s2`, 0.9 GB). MGRS tiles 36SVD and 36SWD, 345 candidate scenes with cloud < 10 %, per tile and season the 8 with the
  least glint over deep water: 64 scenes. Bands B4 and B8 on the 40 m overview, SCL on its 40 m overview.
- **Grid**: sea cells of the app grid within 2.5 km of the Republic's shore, split 4 × 4 (~57 × 69 m): 12 707 cells, 203 312 pixels (water joined to the open sea only: the Larnaca salt lakes are out), 4 seasons,
  one byte each (FNU × 20). 1.10 MB (0.19 MB gzip), loaded only by `PsAreaProfile.load(true)`.
- **Method**: water reflectance at 665 nm = B4 − B8; turbidity = 228.1 r / (1 − r / 0.1641) FNU (Dogliotti et al. 2015, red band; the
  coefficients as in ACOLITE's defaults); seasonal median of the valid scenes (≥ 3) minus the tile's deep-water median (0 to 0.6 FNU);
  only water ≥ 10 m deep is measured (75 % of the pixels), shallower pixels take the nearest measured one within ~600 m (91 % with a value).
  Visibility = `secchiTurbid`: 0.067 1/m of Kd(PAR) per FNU (Devlin et al. 2008 slope on suspended matter, UK coastal water, taking
  1 FNU ≈ 1 g/m³: quoted from memory, not re-read, and not measured in Cyprus). Values under 0.5 FNU count as open water (noise floor).
- **Checks** (`node scripts/areas/check-clarity.mjs` → scripts/areas/validation.json): the reader returns the stored bytes; turbidity falls
  with distance from shore (first 500 m 0.27 / 0.28 / 0.24 / 0.05 FNU winter to autumn, beyond 1.5 km ≤ 0.08); within 1 km of the big river
  mouths 0.19 / 0.14 / 0.21 / 0.05 against 0.15 / 0.13 / 0.16 / 0.04 for the whole first kilometre; rank correlation with the Copernicus
  KD490 climatology on the same 1 km pixels −0.09 / 0.00 / 0.33 / 0.24 (weak); with the EEA sites' enterococci −0.13 (none: bacteria are not
  sediment). With a 7 m depth floor the first 500 m read twice as turbid: bottom glow, hence the 10 m floor.
- **Not settled**: the west coast (tile 36SVD: Pomos to Paphos) reads 16–39 % of its first kilometre "murkier" in spring and summer, the south
  and east 0–3 %. That can be surf on the exposed rocky shore or bottom seen through water the 100 m DTM calls ≥ 10 m, rather than sediment;
  and tile 36SWD had 0.44–0.60 FNU subtracted in summer and autumn, which hides anything smaller there. No in-water turbidity or Secchi
  reading was available to decide. Clear days only: storm run-off and the days after rough seas are not in it.

## R15-D. Satellite-derived depth of the shallow coast (round 15 seabed, phase 1: `data/sdb/`, built by `scripts/seabed-s2/sdb.mjs`, read by `js/sdb.js`)
- **What EMODnet's "measured" depth is here (found this round).** ERDDAP `bathymetry_dtm_2024` (erddap.emodnet.eu, keyless) gives each DTM cell's
  `interpolation_flag`, `value_count` and `cdi_index`. Off the Republic's coast 82-98 % of the cells at 2-20 m are flagged not interpolated, but
  their source (index 19316, ~109 values per ~100 m cell, cut off at 20 m) is the composite "cyprus2002": **EOMAP's Sentinel-2 satellite-derived
  bathymetry of 2019** (sextant record SDN_CPRD_4667_cyprus2002), averaged to the cell. Deeper than 20 m the cells are interpolated (the other
  composite is UKHO chart surveys of the 1930s-50s). So there is no sounding to calibrate on in 0-20 m; the DTM there is another satellite estimate.
- **Independent truth: ICESat-2.** NASA ATL03 photons through the OpenAltimetry API (keyless; ATL24, NASA's own bathymetry product, needs an Earthdata
  login and was not used). 16 ground tracks, 509 overflights 2018-2026, strong beams, only the stretches over water of 30 m or less: 1,300 stretches,
  343 MB in the cache. Own bottom detector (`scripts/seabed-s2/icesat2.mjs`: density above the noise, grown along the track, refraction 0.7477):
  **23,558 laser depths on 589 beam passes** (1.5-5 m 8,131; 5-10 m 10,664; 10-15 m 3,872; 15-20 m 811; 20-30 m 80). None shallower than 1.5 m;
  dark beds return fewer photons, so sand is over-represented. Not tide-corrected (under 0.2 m here). The detector is not NASA's classifier.
- **Method** (`sdb.mjs` header): the 6 calmest, clearest Sentinel-2 L2A scenes per MGRS tile (as `data/seabed-s2.js`), deglinted; per scene the
  bottom signal in blue and green (3 x 3 mean), an optically-deep cut-off (2 sd of the deep water) and a depth limit (16-20 m); Lyzenga log-linear
  depth fitted on the DTM's satellite cells in 5 folds of 10 km blocks; median over scenes; a regional line per 5 km block; half-way fusion with the
  DTM's satellite cells at cell scale; one 9-number correction against the laser. Expected error per pixel from the laser by depth and scene spread.
- **Result against the laser** (held-out: scene fits without the pixel's 10 km block, laser correction without its fold; CI over passes; the DTM is
  bilinear at the same points). Mean absolute error, this map / DTM: all 23,254 points **0.88 / 1.03 m, -0.15 [-0.18, -0.13]**. By the map's own depth:
  0-2 m 0.56 / 1.46; 2-5 m 0.61 / 0.83; 5-10 m 0.79 / 0.92 (-0.13 [-0.17, -0.09]); 10-13 m 1.29 / 1.27 (+0.01 [-0.05, +0.07]); 13-16 m 1.71 / 1.78
  (-0.06 [-0.21, +0.10]); 16-20 m 2.52 / 2.78; over 20 m 6.1 / 6.9 (12 points). Where the DTM is interpolated 0.73 / 1.23; where it is EOMAP's 0.91 / 1.00.
  Sand 0.73 / 0.79, Posidonia 1.07 / 1.33, reef 1.11 / 1.43. By the laser's depth it reads too shallow from 15 m: 15-20 m bias -1.6 m (2.05 / 1.93),
  20-30 m bias -3.4 m (3.42 / 2.20, worse than the DTM). Worse than the DTM in two 15 km tiles of the north-west (Chrysochou bay, t4_4 0.66 / 0.48,
  t4_5 1.09 / 0.93: dark sand); the fusion weight (0.5) was chosen on these same points (overall 0.89 / 0.88 / 0.93 at 0 / 0.5 / 1).
- **Limits.** No estimate where the water is optically deep or the bed dark: 20 % of the water pixels of the set; over Posidonia a quarter at 10-15 m and
  a third at 15-20 m. Trusted by the reader's rule (depth <= 16 m and expected error <= max(1 m, 25 %)): 62 % of the water in the set. Nothing checks
  the first 1.5 m (surf, the shoreline pixel). Clear calm days only. Agreement with the DTM's satellite cells (held-out, before the regional step):
  RMSE 0.7 m at 2-5 m, 1.1 at 5-10, 1.5 at 10-15, 2.0 at 15-20.
- **Files**: 27 tiles of 15 km, 12 x 12 pixels per 250 m cell (~19 x 23 m), depth and expected error in 0.1 m, 3.16 MB of .js (1.41 MB gzip), not
  loaded by index.html. Numbers: `scripts/seabed-s2/sdb-validation.json`. Check: `node scripts/seabed-s2/read.mjs`.

## R15-D2. Seabed classes and structure at 20 m, and where the tiles live (round 15 seabed, phase 2: `scripts/seabed-s2/pack.mjs`, `js/sdb.js`)

  folder to the data repo as `sdb/v1/`. The phase 1 `data/sdb/*.js` tiles are gone (same reader calls).
- **Classes**: the per-pixel probabilities of `scripts/seabed-s2/build.mjs` (the classifier behind `data/seabed-s2.js`: Sentinel-2 composite, Lyzenga
  index, brightness, texture, colour, context; calibrated on EMODnet Seabed Habitats CY005001, CC BY 4.0), averaged over 2 x 2 pixels.
  **Held-out (5 folds of 10 km blocks) against CY005001 at 20 m: 78.5 % agree, kappa 0.62** (304,248 pixels). The survey's Posidonia is found 87 %
  of the time, its sand 79 %, its rock 30 %; a pixel this map calls rock is rock in the survey 48 % of the time (Posidonia 79 %, sand 83 %). By depth: 0-3 m 54 %,
  3-6 m 67 %, 6-10 m 78 %, 10-16 m 83 %, 16-25 m 82 %. The stored probability is honest: 0.9+ agrees 95 %, 0.8-0.9 87 %, 0.7-0.8 76 %,
  0.6-0.7 67 %, 0.5-0.6 56 %, under 0.5 44 %. Against the classes the app has where there is no survey (EUSeaMap, same cells, round 13 numbers

  measure a 20 m edge. **Sand holes** (sand patches of 0.09-1.1 ha ringed >= 70 % by Posidonia): 2,255, mean 0.28 ha; nothing open can check them.

  the Posidonia tail is mostly the dark bed read as depth, not relief, so the reader marks relief on or within 40 m of Posidonia 'doubtful'. The floor
